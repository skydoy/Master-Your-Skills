---
name: email
description: Writes new emails and replies that sound like a person wrote them. Use when the user says "write an email", "draft a reply", "answer this email", "reply to", "how do I respond", "email to my boss/client", or pastes an email and asks what to say.
---

Produce an email the user can paste and send without rewriting it.

## Do this

1. **Pick the kind.**
   - **New:** needs context, goal (what the recipient should do or know), data, recipient.
   - **Reply:** needs the email being answered, earlier thread if any, context, goal, data.
2. **Pick the mode.** Enough to write: write now (Direct). Gaps: ask one numbered round of up to 6 questions, each with a suggested answer in brackets, so "ok" accepts all (Guided). Max 2 rounds. Infer names, roles and language from the thread.
3. **For a reply, read the received email first.**
   - List every question and request in it, even the ones in passing. Each gets an answer, or a clear "I'll check and come back by [DATE]".
   - Mirror the sender: same language, same register (first name or surname, "Hi" or "Dear"), similar length. A three-line email gets a short answer.
   - Reuse their names and terms. If they call it "the Q3 file", do not call it "the quarterly report".
4. **For a new email, write the subject line.** Specific and short: what it is plus what is needed ("Budget 2027: your OK by Friday"). Not "Quick question".
5. **Write the body.**
   - The point or the answer in the first two lines.
   - Then only the context the reader needs to act.
   - One clear ask, with a date if there is one.
   - Short paragraphs; a list only for 3+ parallel items.
   - Close as the user would. No "I hope this finds you well", no "don't hesitate to reach out", no recap paragraph.
6. **Guard the facts.** Never invent dates, prices, names or commitments. Missing ones become `[DATE]`, `[AMOUNT]` and are listed at the end. Do not promise on the user's behalf what they did not say.
7. **Deliver** in the recipient's language:
   - The email, ready to paste (subject first for new emails).
   - A shorter variant only if the user asked for options.
   - Check before sending.

For a one-line reply, skip everything except steps 3 and 7.

## Stop and ask when

- The goal is ambiguous: accept, decline or negotiate lead to different emails.
- The reply would commit the user to money, a deadline or a legal position they did not state.
- The thread suggests conflict and the user's stance (firm or conciliatory) is unknown.

## Done when

- Every question in the received email has an answer or a stated follow-up.
- Language and register match the reader.
- New emails have a specific subject line.
- No invented facts; placeholders listed.

Check before sending lists 2-4 items: placeholders, attachments mentioned, facts or dates to verify, phrases that could read as cold or as a commitment.

## Traps

- Answering the main question and missing the one in the PS.
- Replying formally to a casual sender, or the reverse.
- Replying in the user's language when the thread is in another.
- Padding a simple yes into paragraphs.
