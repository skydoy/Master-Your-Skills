name: summarize-doc
description: Summarises a contract, manual or long email for a specific task: what it means for you, deadlines, obligations, numbers, risks, with page references. Use for "summarise this", "what do I need to do", "resumir contrato", "resumo do documento", "prazos e obrigações".
---

Read the document for the reader's job, not for completeness. Report only what the text says.

## Mode

- **Direct**: the user stated the task ("I must renew this", "what do I owe by when"). Summarise at once.
- **Guided**: no task given. Ask one round, max 6 questions, with suggested answers: your role in this document [the party who signs], what you must decide or do [decide whether to sign], depth [one page], language [same as the user's], anything to watch [deadlines and costs]. Show a 4-line brief before long output.

Reply in the user's language; quote the document in its own language.

## Do this

1. Read the whole text first. If it is cut off or scanned badly, say which parts you could not read.
2. Write the summary in this order, skipping empty sections:
   - **Bottom line**: 2-3 sentences, what it means for me.
   - **What I must do**: each obligation, who, by when.
   - **Deadlines and dates**: date, event, consequence of missing it.
   - **Numbers**: prices, fees, limits, penalties, notice periods, with currency and unit as written.
   - **Risks and unusual clauses**: automatic renewal, penalties, liability, exclusivity, unilateral changes, short notice windows.
   - **What the document does not say**: gaps relevant to the task.
3. Add a reference to every item: page, section or clause number, for example (p. 4, cl. 7.2). For emails, say "paragraph 3" or quote five words.
4. Never fill gaps, estimate, or apply outside rules. If a clause is ambiguous, quote it and say it is ambiguous.
5. Convert nothing silently: if you compute a date (30 days after signing), show the calculation and mark it as yours.

## Stop and ask when

The task depends on a fact outside the document, such as which party the user is.

## Done when

- Each date, number and duty has a reference.
- Nothing appears that the text does not support.
- Risks are tied to the user's task.

## Traps

- Summarising everything evenly; weight by the task.
- Calling a clause "standard" or "fine": that is legal advice. Flag it for a professional when stakes are high.

End with "Check before using": verify the key dates and amounts in the original, and have a lawyer confirm any contract clause marked as a risk.
