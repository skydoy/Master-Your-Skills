Make feedback concrete enough that the person knows what to repeat or change.

## Mode

- The user gave examples and the person's role: Direct.
- Otherwise Guided. One numbered round, max 6 questions, each with a suggested answer in brackets ("ok" accepts all): role and period [last 6 months], 2-3 concrete examples of strengths, 2-3 of growth areas, goals or expectations used, tone [direct and kind], language. Max 2 rounds. Show a 4-6 line brief first.
- If a draft was pasted, go to step 5 first.

## Do this

1. Collect evidence only from what the user gave. No example means [EXAMPLE NEEDED], never a made-up one.
2. Write each point as Situation (when, where), Behaviour (what was seen or said, observable), Impact (effect on result, team or client). One example per point.
3. Strengths: 2-4, each with evidence and why it matters, so the person repeats it.
4. Growth areas: 1-3, each with the behaviour to change, the wanted behaviour instead, and a small first step. Keep strengths and growth balanced.
5. Scan the wording and flag, with a rewrite:
   - Vague: "good attitude", "needs to be more proactive". Ask what was done.
   - Personality labels: "lazy", "aggressive". Replace by the behaviour.
   - Absolutes: "always", "never".
   - Bias signals: comments on age, gender, accent, family, or style ("abrasive" for one group, "assertive" for another); a recent event outweighing the whole period; praise for effort only in some people; comparison with other people instead of with goals.
6. Agreed goals: 2-4 as what, measure, date. Mark any goal the person has not yet agreed to [TO AGREE].
7. Add 2-3 questions to ask the person, so the review is a conversation.

## Rules

- Judge against the role's expectations, not against personality.
- Anything unverified stays out or is marked as hearsay.
- Never write a rating or promotion outcome the user did not state.

## Done when

- Each strength and growth point has a situation, behaviour and impact.
- No flagged wording remains, or the user kept it knowingly.
- Goals have a measure and a date.
- Ends with "Check before using": placeholders, examples confirmed with dates, HR policy and rating scale, whether the wording would be fair for anyone else in the same role.

## Traps

- Recency: one bad week outweighing six months.
- A feedback "sandwich" that hides the real message.
