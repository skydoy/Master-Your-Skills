---

Compress a pile of material into one page a senior reader can act on in two minutes.

## Mode
- **Direct**: material and reader are clear. Write the brief now.
- **Guided**: ask one numbered round, max 6 questions, each with a bracketed suggestion so "yes" accepts all: who decides [the sender's boss], what decision [infer from material], deadline [none], tone [neutral, formal], language [that of the reader], length [1 page]. Then show a 4-6 line brief (goal, audience, tone, format, key facts) before the full text. Max 2 rounds.

## Do this
1. Read everything first. Find the decision hidden in the material; if none exists, say "information only" and name the nearest decision.
2. Fill this page, in this order:
   - **Decision needed**: one sentence, with owner and date.
   - **The 3 facts that matter**: each one line, each changes the decision. Drop the rest.
   - **Numbers**: 3-6 figures with unit, period and source. Compare to target or last period.
   - **Risks**: top 3, each with likelihood, impact and the mitigation.
   - **Recommendation**: one option, one reason, one next step.
   - **If we do nothing**: the concrete consequence and when it bites.
3. Cut: history, method, and anything the reader already knows. Put detail in "Available on request: [list]".
4. Mark each claim as fact (from the source), estimate, or opinion. Opinions belong only in the recommendation.
5. Write in the reader's language; reply to the user in theirs.

## Rules
- Never invent facts, numbers, names, dates or quotes. Gaps become [VALUE] placeholders, listed at the end.
- Lead with the answer, not the background. Short sentences, plain verbs. No "delve", "leverage", em-dash chains, or closing recap.
- Keep numbers exactly as in the source; if two sources disagree, show both and say which you used.

## Stop and ask when
- Sources contradict on the decisive number.
- You cannot tell who the reader is or what they must decide.

## Done when
- The page fits one A4 (about 300 words).
- Every number has a source or a placeholder.
- The decision, the recommendation and the cost of inaction each appear once, in plain words.

## End with
**Check before sending**: placeholders filled; numbers match the sources; recommendation is the sender's view, not just yours; anything sensitive removed.
