description: Turns a task list into a realistic day plan by priority, deadlines, effort and energy, with time blocks, buffers, and what to drop or delegate. Use for "plan my day", "I have too much to do", "organise my tasks", "planear o dia", "organizar tarefas", "prioridades".
---

Build a day that fits in the hours that exist.

## Mode

- **Direct**: the user gave tasks and hours. Plan at once.
- **Guided**: ask one round, max 6 questions, each with a suggested answer: working hours [09:00-17:30]; fixed meetings or calls [none]; deadlines today or tomorrow [?]; estimated time per task if known [I will estimate]; when you have most energy [morning]; who could take something [nobody]. Max 2 rounds. Never ask for what the list already shows.

Reply in the user's language.

## Do this

1. List the tasks. Mark each: deadline, effort (minutes, your estimate if none given, marked as estimate), and type: deep (needs focus), light (email, admin), or waiting (depends on others).
2. Sort: due today and blocking others first; then high value; then the rest. Two tasks of equal weight: the one that unblocks someone goes first.
3. Count capacity: hours available minus meetings, minus 25% for interruptions. Plan to that number, not to the full day.
4. Place blocks: deep work in the high-energy window, light tasks after lunch or in gaps, waiting-tasks started early so the reply arrives while you do other things. Add a 10-15 minute buffer after each long block and a batch slot of 30 minutes for messages.
5. Output a table: time, task, effort, note. Put fixed items first, then the rest.
6. If the load exceeds capacity, say so plainly and split the leftovers into: **drop** (no deadline, low value), **delegate** (who, what to hand over), **move** (new date). Do not squeeze everything in.
7. Name the one task that must be done even if the day collapses.
8. Close with a one-line end-of-day review to fill in: "Done: __ / Moved: __ / Learned: __".

## Done when

- The planned minutes fit the capacity.
- Every task is placed, moved, delegated or dropped.
- A must-do task is named.

## Traps

- Planning 100% of the day; plans break at the first call.
- Underestimating effort: raise a guess by half if the user has no history.
- Hiding the bad news that not everything fits.

End with "Check before using": confirm the estimates, confirm deadlines with their real dates, and check that meetings you do not know about are not missing.
