name: delegate
description: Turns an idea into one clear, ready-to-send request for the team with what, why, deadline, success measure, constraints and who to ask. Use for "delegate this", "write a request for my team", "delegar tarefa", "pedido para a equipe", "assign this task".
---

Write one message that the receiver can act on without a follow-up call.

## Mode

- The user gave the task and the deadline: Direct. Produce the message at once.
- Otherwise Guided. One numbered round, max 6 questions, each with a suggested answer in brackets ("ok" accepts all): who receives it, the outcome wanted, deadline [DATE], how you will know it is good, limits (budget, tools, approvals), who they can ask [me]. Max 2 rounds. Show a 4-6 line brief first.
- Never ask what you can infer from the idea.

## Do this

1. Write the outcome, not the method: what exists when it is done. Leave the how to the receiver unless a method is a real constraint.
2. Give the why in 1-2 sentences: who needs it and what it changes. Motivation changes the quality of the work.
3. Set the deadline with a date, plus a checkpoint for anything over a week ("show me a first version on [DATE]").
4. Define success as something checkable: a number, a document with named sections, or a yes from a named person. "Good quality" is not a measure.
5. List constraints: budget, tools, standards, things not to touch, approvals needed.
6. Say how much freedom they have: decide alone, check with me first, or I decide.
7. Name who to ask for what: questions to the delegator, data to a named colleague.
8. Close with the first step you expect, or a simple "tell me by [DATE] if this is not feasible".

## Message format

Short, in the language and register of the receiver (a colleague, not a form). Subject line plus 120-200 words. A short list is fine for success measures and constraints. No filler openings, no closing summary.

Example shape:
"Subject: Customer survey summary by Fri 12 Dec. [Name], ..."

## Rules

- Missing facts become [DATE], [NAME], [VALUE] and are listed after the message.
- Never promise budget, authority or time the user did not state.
- Leave out guesses about the person's skills.

## Done when

- The message has outcome, reason, date, measure, constraints and contact.
- It stands alone: someone reading it cold understands the task.
- Ends with "Check before sending": placeholders, deadline realistic for their workload, anyone who should be copied.

## Traps

- Describing steps and hiding the goal.
- A deadline of "soon" or "ASAP".
- Sending it with no way to ask questions.
