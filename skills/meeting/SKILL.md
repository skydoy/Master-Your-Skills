Make meetings short before and useful after. Two modes: agenda (before) and minutes (after).

## Choose the mode and how to ask

- Notes or a transcript were given: minutes mode, Direct. Produce at once.
- A topic or purpose was given: agenda mode. Direct if you have topic, length and attendee roles; otherwise Guided.
- Guided: ask one numbered round, max 6 questions, each with a suggested answer in brackets so "ok" accepts all (purpose [decide X], length [30 min], who attends, what must be decided, pre-reads, language). Max 2 rounds. Then show a 4-6 line brief (goal, audience, tone, length, key facts) before the long output.

## Agenda

1. State the meeting goal in one sentence: a decision, a plan or an information exchange. If no outcome is possible, suggest an email instead.
2. List items. Each gets: a goal verb (decide, agree, inform, brainstorm), a time box, and a lead person if known.
3. Time boxes add up to the length minus 10% buffer. Put decisions first, status last.
4. Add "Prepare before": what each person reads or brings.

## Minutes

1. Open with date, attendees and absentees exactly as the source gives them. Missing names stay [ATTENDEES].
2. Decisions: only what the source shows as agreed. Each in one line with the reason if stated.
3. Actions table: action, owner, deadline. No owner or date in the source means [OWNER] or [DATE], never a guess.
4. Open questions: items raised but not resolved, with who could answer.
5. Short discussion summary only if it explains a decision. Keep it under 5 lines.
6. A suggestion, preference or "we should" is not a decision. Put it under open questions.
7. Write in the language of the meeting; reply to the user in theirs.

## Stop and ask when

The transcript is ambiguous on who owns an action or whether something was agreed. Ask in one line instead of choosing.

## Done when

- The agenda has a goal, time box and total time that fit.
- Every decision and action traces to the source text.
- Placeholders are visible and listed.
- Ends with "Check before sending": placeholders filled, decisions confirmed with the people involved, names spelled right.

## Traps

- Agendas that list topics but no wanted outcome.
- Inventing a deadline to make the table look complete.
- Turning a polite "maybe" into a decision.
