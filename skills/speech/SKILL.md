Write words that sound right out loud, fit the time slot, and leave the audience with one clear change.

## Do this

1. **Check the brief.** You need: occasion, venue, duration, audience, goal (what they should think, feel or do afterwards), tone, and the speaker's own stories or facts. All present: write now (Direct). Gaps: ask one numbered round of up to 6 questions, each with a suggested answer in brackets, so "ok" accepts all (Guided). A second round only if an answer opens a new gap.
2. **Show the brief** in Guided mode only: 4-6 lines with goal, audience, tone, length, key stories. Wait for OK.
3. **Size it.** Target words = minutes × 130, within ±10%. A 3-minute toast is about 390 words.
4. **Pick one message.** Write it as one sentence. Every paragraph must serve it; cut what does not.
5. **Build the shape.**
   - Opening: a concrete image, story, question or fact. No thanks-list first, no "Today I want to talk about".
   - Body: 2-4 points max. Signpost each one ("First...", "Here is the second thing...") because listeners cannot scroll back.
   - Close: a line that echoes the opening, so the talk feels finished. Then stop.
6. **Write for the ear.**
   - Short sentences, mostly under 15 words. One idea per paragraph.
   - Plain spoken words; contractions are fine. Repeat key phrases on purpose.
   - Round numbers and add a comparison ("one in four of us").
   - Mark pauses as `[pause]` after key lines and before the close.
   - Use only the speaker's real stories and facts. Anything missing becomes `[STORY: ...]` or `[FACT]`.
7. **Read it at speaking pace.** Fix any sentence that needs a mid-breath or trips the tongue.
8. **Deliver** in the audience's language:
   - The speech.
   - Timing: word count and estimated minutes at 130 wpm.
   - 3 delivery notes specific to this speech (where to slow down, where to look up, what to do with the pause).
   - Check before using.

Short toast or a few remarks: skip the brief and the signposting; keep opening, one point, echo close.

## Stop and ask when

- The goal is unclear: "inform" and "persuade" make different speeches.
- The speaker's role is unknown (host, guest, boss, best man) and it changes the register.
- The content clearly overruns the time; ask what to cut.

## Done when

- Word count is within ±10% of minutes × 130.
- The closing line echoes the opening.
- Every fact and story came from the user or is a visible placeholder.
- Timing, 3 delivery notes and the check list are present.

Check before using lists 2-4 items: placeholders, names to pronounce, facts to verify, jokes that could land badly.

## Traps

- Essay prose: nested clauses that read well and sound tired.
- A joke about someone in the room they would not welcome.
- Ending on a summary ("So in conclusion..."). End on the echo line.
- Invented anecdotes; the speaker will be asked about them.
